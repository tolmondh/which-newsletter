beehiiv vs Kit vs Ghost vs Substack

A practical matrix for creators choosing where to publish and grow a newsletter. Framed for September 2026. Pricing and features change — verify on each vendor’s site before you commit.

Pricing shape (not a quote sheet)

Exact dollar amounts move often. Use this as a shape check, then confirm on each site:

  • beehiiv — Free tier for starting; paid tiers scale with subscribers and unlock growth/monetization features. Expect costs to rise with list size.
  • Kit — Free for small lists; paid plans based primarily on subscriber count. Creator commerce features sit on top of email.
  • Ghost — Ghost(Pro) is a flat hosting fee by tier (members/traffic), or self-host and pay your own infra + email sending. Stripe fees on paid memberships.
  • Substack — Free to publish; Substack takes a percentage of paid subscription revenue (plus payment processing). Low friction, higher take-rate if you scale paid.

Always model: list size × plan, payment processor fees, and any ad-network or referral revenue — not just the sticker price.

Platform snapshots

Affiliate candidate

beehiiv

Built for newsletter operators who care about growth and sponsorship-style monetization. Strong on referrals, ads, and “media company” workflows. Less of a full website CMS than Ghost; more growth tooling than Substack’s simple writer UX.

Best when: you want list growth features and built-in ad/sponsorship paths without bolting everything on yourself.

Watch for: pricing as you scale; confirm which growth features sit behind which plan.

Affiliate candidate

Kit (formerly ConvertKit)

Email marketing for creators: tagging, visual automations, landing pages, and commerce hooks. Feels like an ESP first, publication second. Strong fit if you sell digital products or courses and need sequences.

Best when: your newsletter is part of a funnel — free content → tags → offers → checkout.

Watch for: you’ll usually still want a separate site or course platform; Kit isn’t Ghost.

Affiliate candidate

Ghost

Open-source publishing stack: beautiful sites, newsletters, and memberships via Stripe. You own the stack (especially if you self-host). Fewer “growth hacks,” more control and a real CMS.

Best when: you want a branded publication site, paid members, and long-term ownership — and you’re willing to own more of the setup.

Watch for: deliverability and growth are more DIY; no Substack-style network discovery.

Substack

The simplest path to write, build a paid subscription, and tap network recommendations. Not a full marketing automation platform. Great for writers; less ideal if you need heavy tagging, courses, or a custom site.

Best when: you want to start fast, lean on discovery, and charge readers with minimal ops.

Watch for: revenue share on paid; limited automations; brand lives inside Substack’s product surface.

Quick chooser

  • Want ads + growth tooling in one newsletter product? Start with beehiiv.
  • Selling courses/products through email automations? Start with Kit.
  • Want your own site + memberships? Start with Ghost.
  • Want the simplest paid writing + network? Start with Substack.
