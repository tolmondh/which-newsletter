How to monetize a newsletter on beehiiv

beehiiv gives you several ways to earn from one list: its ad network, Boosts (getting paid to recommend other newsletters), paid subscriptions, sponsors you sell yourself, and digital products. The trick is sequencing them so you don’t burn reader trust before the list is big enough to pay. This is a process map, not an income promise — verify current eligibility rules, plan gates, and payout terms in beehiiv’s docs.

Quick answer

  • Start with growth, not ads. Every lever pays more with a bigger, more engaged list, so referrals and consistent publishing come first.
  • Add low-effort revenue next: the ad network and Boosts can run alongside your writing without you selling anything.
  • Layer higher-effort revenue later: paid tiers, direct sponsors, and digital products usually pay more per reader but need a clear offer and audience trust.

A sensible order to turn them on

  1. Publish consistently and turn on referrals Pick a schedule you can keep, and set up beehiiv’s referral program so readers bring readers. Monetization works on top of a list, not instead of one.
  2. Apply for the ad network and Boosts These are the lowest-effort revenue sources. Review each ad or Boost offer for audience fit rather than accepting everything.
  3. Watch engagement before adding more If open or click rates drop after you add ads, scale back. Engagement is what advertisers and paying readers both care about.
  4. Launch a paid tier when you have a premium promise Something concrete (extra issues, deep dives, a community, templates) that free readers can see they’re missing. Our paid newsletter setup guide walks through it.
  5. Sell sponsors directly once you know your niche’s buyers A media kit with your real, current numbers (never inflated) and a simple placement menu is enough to start.

Honest tradeoffs

  • Convenience vs control: the ad network and Boosts are hands-off, but beehiiv sets the marketplace rules and terms. Direct sponsors pay on your terms but take sales time.
  • Free vs paid content: paywalling your best work can raise revenue per reader while slowing list growth, which in turn shrinks ad and Boost income. Many newsletters keep the core free and charge for extras.
  • Reader trust: every lever spends a little trust. Recommend only what fits, label sponsored content, and keep ad load reasonable.
  • Platform dependence: your subscriber list exports, but marketplace income (ads and Boosts) stays on beehiiv if you leave. Paid subscribers on Stripe and direct sponsors are more portable.

Common mistakes

  • Monetizing before there’s a habit. Ads on an irregular newsletter tend to underperform; consistency first.
  • Accepting every offer. Off-topic ads and Boosts train readers to ignore your recommendations.
  • A paid tier with no clear extra. “Support my work” works for some writers, but a concrete premium benefit is easier to sell.
  • Inflated media kits. Use real, current numbers. Sponsors check, and renewals depend on actual results.
  • Skipping disclosure. Label sponsored and affiliate content; it’s both an FTC rule and good for trust.

What to do next

  1. Pick your first revenue lever For most new lists, that’s the ad network or Boosts, once you’re eligible.
  2. Confirm eligibility and plan gates Read beehiiv’s current docs for that feature and check which plan it needs.
  3. Set a trust budget Decide how many ads or recommendations per issue you’ll allow, and stick to it.
  4. Review after a month Compare revenue against open and click trends, then decide whether to add a paid tier or direct sponsors.
