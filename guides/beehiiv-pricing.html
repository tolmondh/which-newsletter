beehiiv pricing: Launch vs Scale vs Max

You’re deciding whether to stay on free Launch or pay for Scale/Max. This page is a plan-shape guide — not a live price sheet. Confirm dollars and limits on beehiiv before you upgrade.

Pricing moves. As of September 2026, Launch is free (subscriber cap applies), and paid plans start in the ballpark of tens of dollars per month and scale with list size. Always verify on beehiiv’s pricing page. Affiliate link — see disclosure.

Plan shape at a glance

Plan Cost shape Typical unlock
Launch $0 / month · subscriber cap (commonly ~2,500) Core newsletter, website, sending — start without a card
Scale Paid · rises with subscribers Monetization & growth stack (ads, paid subs tools, automations, analytics)
Max Higher paid tier White-label / remove branding, more publications, dynamic content, priority support

All plans (including Launch) are generally positioned with unlimited email sends — you’re paying for features and list size, not per-send metering. Confirm that still holds on the live pricing page.

Launch (free) — who it’s for

Stay on Launch if:

  • You’re validating the newsletter idea or migrating a small segment first.
  • You’re under the free subscriber cap and don’t need paid-tier monetization tools yet.
  • You’re comparing beehiiv against Substack/Kit before committing a full list.

Outgrow Launch when: you hit the subscriber limit, or you need ad network / paid subscription tooling / advanced analytics that sit behind paid plans.

Scale — who should pay

Scale is the default paid plan for creators who are serious about the newsletter as a business line.

Pay for Scale when at least one is true:

  • You want beehiiv’s ad network / sponsorship tooling live, not hypothetical.
  • You’re turning on paid subscriptions and want the platform’s paid-sub tooling (and the 0% platform cut positioning vs Substack’s take-rate).
  • You need automations and deeper analytics as part of a weekly publishing rhythm.
  • You’ve outgrown Launch’s subscriber cap.

Don’t upgrade “just because.” If you’re still figuring out niche and cadence, Launch is enough.

Max — when the jump is worth it

Max is for operators who need polish and capacity — not for day-one publishers.

Consider Max when:

  • Removing beehiiv branding (newsletter + site) matters for client or brand work.
  • You run multiple publications and need higher publication limits than Scale.
  • You want dynamic content, priority human support, or other Max-only upgrades beehiiv lists on pricing.

If branding and multi-pub aren’t pain points, Scale is usually the better value.

Upgrade decision checklist

  1. Count real subscribers Active list size — not vanity social followers.
  2. Name the feature you’re buying Ads? Paid subs? Branding removal? Cap headroom? If you can’t name it, don’t upgrade yet.
  3. Model annual cost vs revenue path Plan fee vs expected sponsorships or paid-sub margin. Include Stripe fees.
  4. Confirm on beehiiv’s site Limits, feature gates, and current dollar amounts change.

vs Substack’s “free + take-rate”

Substack feels free until paid subscriptions work — then the percentage cut matters. beehiiv charges software (free → paid tiers) and positions paid subscription revenue as yours minus Stripe. Neither is automatically cheaper: small paid pubs on Substack can be fine; larger paid or ad-driven pubs often model better on beehiiv. See beehiiv vs Substack.

Start free on Launch, unlock Scale when monetization or list size demands it, and only buy Max for branding/multi-pub needs.
