beehiiv vs AWeber

AWeber is one of the original email marketing tools, and plenty of small businesses still run their follow-up sequences on it. beehiiv is built for a different job: a newsletter that is itself the product, with a publication website, referral growth, and built-in ways to earn from the list. So the real question is whether your email is a sales follow-up machine or a media product. This guide maps the tradeoffs honestly. Quick verdict

  • Pick beehiiv if the newsletter is the product: you publish on a cadence, want referral and recommendation growth, and plan to earn from ads, Boosts, sponsors, or paid subscriptions.
  • Pick AWeber if email mainly exists to nurture leads and sell your own products or services, and your autoresponder sequences are the part that makes money.
  • Neither is “better.” The wrong pick costs you either newsletter growth and revenue levers, or sales sequences you’d have to rebuild elsewhere.

When beehiiv fits

Choose beehiiv when the regular issue is the center of the business, not a vehicle for a sales sequence.

  • You want newsletter-native growth (referrals, recommendations, Boosts) inside the product rather than relying only on forms and ads.
  • You plan to treat ads, sponsorships, or paid subscriptions as real revenue lines. See how to monetize a newsletter on beehiiv.
  • You want a publication website and archive so every issue is readable and findable on the web.
  • Your AWeber account has drifted into “we send a weekly newsletter” and the long follow-up series rarely change anymore.

When AWeber fits

Stay on (or choose) AWeber when email is a sales and follow-up channel and your revenue comes from what you sell, not from the newsletter.

  • You run lead magnets and multi-step follow-up series that convert subscribers into clients or buyers.
  • You sell courses, coaching, services, or info products and rely on tag-based automations to route people.
  • You value a long-established vendor and its support style, and your team already knows the tool.
  • Your current setup works, so a migration would cost time without unlocking a revenue lever you’d actually use.

Honest flip side: AWeber is built for follow-up, but referral growth loops, a newsletter-style publication site, and ad or sponsor revenue are things you’d build or skip yourself.

Switching from AWeber to beehiiv

If beehiiv fits better, treat the move as a short project. Process map (confirm each step against current beehiiv and AWeber help docs):

  1. Export Currently Subscribed people from AWeber Export each list’s subscribed contacts as CSV. Leave pending (unconfirmed) and unsubscribed contacts behind so they’re never re-imported.
  2. Set up the beehiiv publication first Branding, sender name, reply-to, issue template, and the welcome email, all before any import.
  3. Authenticate your sending domain Add the DNS records beehiiv asks for. Leave existing AWeber records in place until cutover is done.
  4. Import and tag Merge multiple AWeber lists into one beehiiv publication, using tags or segments to keep track of where people came from.
  5. Rebuild must-keep follow-ups and forms Recreate only the sequences that still earn their keep, then swap AWeber forms and landing page links on your site and bios for beehiiv signup forms.
  6. Test, then send one cutover issue Send tests to your own inboxes, then a normal issue telling readers you’ve moved. Keep your AWeber export until you’re confident nothing is missing.
  • Never re-permission people who unsubscribed or never confirmed on AWeber.
