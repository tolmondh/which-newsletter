beehiiv vs Buttondown

Buttondown is a small, independent, Markdown-first newsletter tool that writers and developers love for its simplicity. beehiiv is built around the newsletter as a media business: a publication site, referral growth, and built-in monetization. Both send a clean newsletter, so the real question is whether you want a quiet writing tool or a growth engine. This guide maps the tradeoffs without sales-page fluff. Verify current plan names, limits, and feature gates on each vendor’s site before you commit.

Quick verdict

  • Pick beehiiv if you want the newsletter to grow and pay: referral rewards, recommendations, Boosts, an ad network, sponsors, or paid tiers, all inside one product.
  • Pick Buttondown if you mainly want to write in Markdown and hit send, value a lightweight indie tool, or want API and RSS hooks without a lot of interface.
  • Neither is “better.” The wrong tool costs you either growth and monetization leverage or the calm, minimal workflow you actually enjoy.

When beehiiv fits

Choose beehiiv when the newsletter is meant to become a real audience business, not just a personal dispatch.

  • You want growth built in (referral program, recommendations, Boosts) instead of relying only on word of mouth and an embed form.
  • You plan to earn from ads, sponsorships, or paid subscriptions and want those workflows in the same place you write. See how to monetize a newsletter on beehiiv.
  • You want a publication website with design controls, so the archive looks like a media brand rather than a plain list of posts.
  • You’ve outgrown Buttondown because you keep bolting on outside tools for referrals, sponsor slots, or landing pages.

When Buttondown fits

Stay on (or choose) Buttondown when simplicity is the feature you’re paying for.

  • You write in Markdown and want an editor that stays out of the way.
  • You’re a developer or technical writer who wants an API, webhooks, or RSS-to-email to automate sending from a blog or repo.
  • Your newsletter is personal or small by design, and you don’t need ad networks, Boosts, or referral rewards.
  • You like supporting a small independent tool and the current setup already does the job, so switching costs time and gains little.

Honest flip side: minimal means you build growth and monetization yourself. That’s fine for a hobby or personal list, but more work if you want the newsletter to pay.

Switching from Buttondown to beehiiv

A process map, not a guarantee. Follow each vendor’s current import and export docs.

  1. Export subscribers from Buttondown Download your subscriber list as a CSV, including tags, custom fields, and subscribe dates you want to keep. Export only active subscribers.
  2. Save your archive Export or copy your past emails. Because Buttondown is Markdown-first, keep the source files handy; you may need to reformat images and embeds in beehiiv’s editor.
  3. Create your beehiiv publication Set name, branding, sender details, and the publication site before importing anyone.
  4. Import subscribers Upload the CSV and map fields and tags. Expect a review step for larger or older lists, and only import people who opted in.
  5. Rebuild automations and integrations Recreate your welcome email and any API, webhook, or RSS-driven workflows. Not every Buttondown automation has a one-to-one equivalent, so check what beehiiv supports on your plan.
  6. Handle paid subscribers carefully If you sell paid subscriptions through Stripe, read both vendors’ docs on moving paying members before you promise anything. Billing mistakes cost trust fast.
  7. Update signup points and send one cutover issue Swap embed forms, links in bio, and your site’s signup box to beehiiv. Send tests to yourself, then a normal issue that briefly tells readers you’ve moved. Keep Buttondown active until you’re sure nothing is missing.
  • Never re-permission people who unsubscribed or bounced on Buttondown.
  • Custom domain: if your archive lives on your own domain, plan DNS changes and redirects so old issue links don’t break.
