beehiiv vs Flodesk

Flodesk wins a lot of creators on looks: its emails and forms are beautiful with very little effort. beehiiv wins on what happens after you hit send: a publication website, referral growth, and built-in ways to earn from the newsletter itself. So the real question isn’t which one is prettier, it’s whether your email is a brand storefront or a media product. This guide maps the tradeoffs honestly. Quick verdict

  • Pick beehiiv if the newsletter is the product: you publish on a cadence, want referral and recommendation growth, and plan to earn from ads, Boosts, sponsors, or paid subscriptions.
  • Pick Flodesk if email is a beautiful extension of your brand that mainly sells your own offers (sessions, products, courses) and design matters more than growth tooling.
  • Neither is “better.” The wrong pick costs you either newsletter growth and revenue levers, or the effortless on-brand design you’d have to recreate elsewhere.

When beehiiv fits

Choose beehiiv when the regular issue is the center of the business, not a pretty promo email for a shop.

  • You want newsletter-native growth (referrals, recommendations, Boosts) inside the product rather than relying only on forms and social traffic.
  • You plan to treat ads, sponsorships, or paid subscriptions as real revenue lines. See how to monetize a newsletter on beehiiv.
  • You want a publication website and archive so every issue is readable and findable on the web.
  • You’ve noticed you spend more time polishing layouts than growing readers, and you’d trade some design freedom for growth and revenue tools.

When Flodesk fits

Stay on (or choose) Flodesk when email is part of your brand experience and your revenue comes from what you sell, not from the newsletter.

  • You’re a photographer, coach, designer, or boutique brand and on-brand, magazine-style emails are part of how you win clients.
  • You sell your own products, sessions, or digital downloads and like having forms, emails, and simple checkout in one place.
  • You send occasional launches and nurture sequences rather than a scheduled publication.
  • Your current setup works and your list is growing fine, so a migration would cost time without unlocking a revenue lever you’d actually use.

Honest flip side: Flodesk makes great-looking email easy, but growth loops, a web archive, and ad or sponsor revenue are things you’d build or skip yourself.

Switching from Flodesk to beehiiv

If beehiiv fits better, treat the move as a short project. Process map (confirm each step against current beehiiv and Flodesk help docs):

  1. Export subscribers from Flodesk Export your segments as CSV. Keep only active subscribers for import, and set aside unsubscribed, bounced, and spam-marked contacts so they’re never re-imported.
  2. Set up the beehiiv publication first Branding, sender name, reply-to, a template that echoes your Flodesk look, and the welcome email, all before any import.
  3. Authenticate your sending domain Add the DNS records beehiiv asks for. Leave existing Flodesk records in place until cutover is done.
  4. Import and tag Map Flodesk segments to beehiiv tags or segments so you can still target the same groups.
  5. Rebuild workflows and forms Recreate the welcome and must-keep sequences, then swap Flodesk forms and landing page links on your site and social bios for beehiiv signup forms.
  6. Plan for checkout separately If you sell through Flodesk Checkout, decide where those sales will live before cancelling anything, and don’t promise buyers continuity you haven’t confirmed.
  7. Test, then send one cutover issue Send tests to your own inboxes, then a normal issue telling readers you’ve moved. Keep your Flodesk export until you’re confident nothing is missing.
  • Never re-permission people who unsubscribed or bounced on Flodesk.
