Quick verdict

  • Pick beehiiv if the newsletter is the product: publish on a cadence, grow the list with referrals/boosts, sell ads/sponsorships, and optionally paid subscriptions.
  • Pick Mailchimp if email is one channel in a broader marketing stack: audiences, campaigns, ecommerce CRM-ish workflows, and agency-style ops you already know.
  • Neither is “better” — wrong tool costs you either newsletter growth leverage or marketing-automation depth.

When beehiiv fits

Choose beehiiv when the weekly/biweekly issue is the center of the business — not a drip campaign bolted onto a store.

  • You want newsletter-native growth (referrals, boosts, recommendation surfaces) closer to the product than DIY forms + ads alone.
  • You care about ads/sponsorships and/or paid subscriptions as first-class monetization, not just “send the blast.”
  • You prefer a publication workflow (write → publish → grow) over managing audiences, campaigns, and journeys as separate marketing objects.
  • You’re leaving Mailchimp because pricing, complexity, or “ESP overhead” outweighs the automation depth you actually use.

When Mailchimp fits

Stay on (or choose) Mailchimp when email is infrastructure for broader marketing — not the product itself.

  • You need mature audience/segment and journey tooling for ecommerce, lead magnets, and multi-step marketing beyond a publication cadence.
  • Your team already runs campaigns, reports, and agency workflows inside Mailchimp and switching cost is high.
  • You want a general-purpose ESP that plugs into stores, ads, and CRM-ish stacks more than newsletter growth loops.
  • You are not planning to lean on newsletter ad networks, boosts, or paid-sub publication features anytime soon.

Honest flip side: Mailchimp’s breadth is also why many newsletter-only creators feel overcharged or overcomplicated for “just send the issue.”
