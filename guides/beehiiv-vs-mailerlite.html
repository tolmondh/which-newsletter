beehiiv vs MailerLite

Both have free tiers and both send a good-looking newsletter, so the choice comes down to what the email is for. beehiiv is built around the newsletter as a media product: publishing, referral growth, and built-in monetization. MailerLite is a budget-friendly, all-round email marketing tool: forms, landing pages, automations, and campaigns for a business or creator. This guide maps the tradeoffs without sales-page fluff. Verify current plan names, limits, and feature gates on each vendor’s site before you commit.

Quick verdict

  • Pick beehiiv if the newsletter is the product: you publish on a cadence, want referral and recommendation growth, and plan to earn from ads, Boosts, sponsors, or paid subscriptions.
  • Pick MailerLite if email supports something else (a shop, a service, a course, a local business) and you mainly need affordable forms, landing pages, and automations.
  • Neither is “better.” The wrong tool costs you either newsletter growth and monetization leverage or flexible marketing automation for a small budget.

When beehiiv fits

Choose beehiiv when the weekly or biweekly issue is the center of the business, not a promo email bolted onto a store.

  • You want newsletter-native growth (referrals, recommendations, Boosts) inside the product instead of stitching together forms and ads.
  • You plan to treat ads, sponsorships, or paid subscriptions as real revenue lines. See how to monetize a newsletter on beehiiv.
  • You want a publication website and archive that comes with the newsletter, so posts are readable on the web without a separate site.
  • You’re outgrowing MailerLite because you’re rebuilding newsletter features (archives, referral rewards, sponsor slots) by hand.

When MailerLite fits

Stay on (or choose) MailerLite when email is a tool for a business, not the business itself.

  • You mostly send announcements, promotions, or lead-nurture sequences for a shop, service, or course.
  • You want landing pages, pop-ups, and automations in one inexpensive tool and don’t care about ad networks or referral programs.
  • You rely on integrations with your store or site builder that are already wired up and working.
  • Your list is small, your budget is tight, and the current setup does the job, so switching costs you time and gains little.

Honest flip side: MailerLite’s flexibility means you build the newsletter experience yourself. That’s fine for a business list, but more work if the newsletter is the product.

Price out your list size and must-have features on both current pricing pages, and include revenue features in the math: a tool that costs a bit more but has a revenue lever you’ll actually use may come out ahead, and one you won’t use is just cost.

Switching from MailerLite to beehiiv

If you’ve decided beehiiv fits better, treat the switch as a short project rather than a one-click import. Process map (confirm each step against current beehiiv and MailerLite help docs):

  1. Export active subscribers from MailerLite Export as CSV with the fields you need (email, name, signup date, custom fields). Export unsubscribed and bounced contacts separately so you don’t re-import them as active.
  2. Set up the beehiiv publication first Branding, sender name, reply-to, and the welcome email, before any import.
  3. Authenticate your sending domain Add the DNS records beehiiv asks for so mail sends from your domain. If you used MailerLite domain authentication, leave those records in place until cutover is done.
  4. Import and tag Import the active CSV and tag or segment by source so you can tell migrated readers from new signups.
  5. Rebuild automations, don’t expect a 1:1 transfer Recreate the welcome series and any must-keep flows. Complex marketing automations may not have a direct equivalent; decide what you actually need.
  6. Swap forms and links Replace MailerLite embed forms, pop-ups, and landing page links on your site and social bios with beehiiv signup forms.
  7. Test, then send one cutover issue Send tests to your own inboxes, then a normal issue that briefly tells readers you’ve moved. Keep MailerLite active (or exported) until you’re confident nothing is missing.
  • Never re-permission people who unsubscribed or bounced on MailerLite.
  • Paid content sold through MailerLite or Stripe needs its own plan. Check how subscriptions or customers can move before you promise anything to paying readers.

What to do next

  3. Check both pricing pages for your list size Use vendor docs as of today, not roundups.
  4. If beehiiv wins, start free and migrate deliberately Follow the switching map above, one step at a time.
