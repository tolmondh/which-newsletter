1. Decide if a move fits

Constant Contact is a general email marketing tool aimed at small businesses and organizations: promotional campaigns, event invitations, contact management, and related marketing features. beehiiv is built around publishing a newsletter, with a hosted publication site and web archive, plus growth and monetization tools such as referrals, recommendations, ads, and paid subscriptions.

A move usually makes sense when:

  • Most of what you send is a recurring newsletter, not sales promotions or event invites.
  • You want each issue to live on the web as a post people can find and share.
  • You want built-in ways to grow the list or earn from it, rather than only sending to the contacts you already have.
  • Your current plan’s cost no longer matches how you actually use it. Compare both vendors’ current pricing pages for your list size.

Stay on Constant Contact if you rely heavily on features beehiiv doesn’t center on, such as event registration, e-commerce or point-of-sale integrations, SMS, or a CRM-style contact history your team uses daily. Some businesses keep Constant Contact for promotions and use beehiiv only for the newsletter.

2. Honest caveats

  • Unsubscribes must stay unsubscribed. Export only contacts whose email status is subscribed. Never re-add people who unsubscribed, bounced, or were removed.
  • Automations don’t port. Welcome series, birthday or anniversary emails, and other automated flows need to be rebuilt by hand in beehiiv, if beehiiv supports them on your plan.
  • Templates don’t port. You’ll rebuild your look in beehiiv’s editor. Plan an hour or two for a clean template.
  • History stays behind. Past campaign reports and engagement history generally don’t transfer, so download any reports you want to keep.
  • Imports may be reviewed. beehiiv may review larger imports; follow its current import guidance and expect a short wait.

3. Take inventory first

Before you export anything, write down:

  • Every list you send to, and which ones are really the newsletter versus promotions or one-off events.
  • Any tags or custom fields you actually use for targeting.
  • Every active automation and what it sends.
  • Every place people sign up: website forms, pop-ups, landing pages, social profiles, in-store or event sign-up sheets, and integrations that add contacts automatically.
  • Which domain you send from, and who controls its DNS.

4. Export contacts from Constant Contact

Constant Contact lets you export contacts to a spreadsheet file from the Contacts area. Check its current help docs for the exact steps and which fields you can include.

  • Export the newsletter lists you decided to move, with email status, list membership, and the custom fields you use.
  • Filter the file down to subscribed contacts only. Leave out unsubscribed, bounced, and removed contacts.
  • Keep a column for the original list name so you can tag it later.
  • Store the raw export somewhere offline and private before you change anything; it contains personal data.

5. Import into beehiiv

  1. Create the publication first Branding, sender name, reply-to address, and a short welcome email that mentions your business name so readers recognize you.
  2. Import the cleaned CSV Follow beehiiv’s current import steps and any review it requires.
  3. Tag the source Add a tag like “from-constant-contact”, and carry the old list name over as a custom field or tag.
  4. Spot-check counts Compare imported totals with your cleaned export before you send anything.

6. Lists, tags, and segments

Constant Contact organizes people into lists, while beehiiv treats your audience as one publication’s subscribers that you can segment. A simple mapping:

  • One main newsletter list becomes your beehiiv subscriber base.
  • Extra lists you still need become tags or custom fields, then segments in beehiiv.
  • Lists you only used for one-off events or promotions can stay behind or be archived.

If someone is on several lists, deduplicate by email so they come across once with all the right tags.

7. Automations and templates

  • Rebuild the welcome email first; it’s what every new subscriber sees.
  • Recreate only the automations that still earn their keep. Check beehiiv’s docs for what automation features your plan includes.
  • Rebuild one clean issue template with your logo, colors, and footer, including the physical mailing address that anti-spam rules expect in commercial email.

8. Swap signup forms

  • Replace Constant Contact signup forms on your website with a beehiiv embed form or a link to your beehiiv signup page.
  • Update pop-ups, landing pages, social profile links, and email signatures.
  • Disconnect or redirect any integrations that still add contacts to Constant Contact, so new signups don’t land in the old account.
  • Test a signup yourself, from the form to the welcome email.

9. Domain and sending setup

  • If you send from your own domain, add the SPF/DKIM records beehiiv asks for, plus a DMARC record if you don’t have one. Keep a single SPF record per domain.
  • Remove the old provider’s authentication records only after you’ve stopped sending from Constant Contact.
  • Use the same sender name readers already know.
  • For larger lists, send to your most engaged readers first and follow beehiiv’s warm-up guidance.

10. Cutover

  1. Send a heads-up from Constant Contact Tell readers the newsletter is moving, when, and what sender name to expect.
  2. Final export Export again right before the switch to catch recent signups and unsubscribes.
  3. Switch forms the same day So no new signups land in the old account.
  4. Send your first beehiiv issue Watch opens, bounces, and replies for the first few sends.
  5. Downgrade or cancel last Keep the old account until your exports and reports are saved and nothing still depends on it. Check Constant Contact’s current billing terms before you cancel.

Final checklist

  • Lists, automations, forms, and integrations inventoried
  • Contacts exported, filtered to subscribed only, deduplicated, and backed up offline
  • Imported into beehiiv and tagged “from-constant-contact”
  • Welcome email, template, and needed automations rebuilt
  • All signup forms and integrations switched and tested
  • Sending domain authenticated (if using your own domain)
  • Reports you want to keep downloaded before canceling

If your Constant Contact sends are really a newsletter and you want web posts, growth, and monetization tools in one place, beehiiv is the path this guide assumes. If you depend on events, SMS, or e-commerce marketing, staying put or running both may be the better call.
