1. Decide if migration is worth it

Kit is a creator ESP: tags, sequences, visual automations, creator commerce hooks, and deep audience tooling. beehiiv is newsletter-first: publishing cadence, growth loops, referrals, ads/boosts, and paid subscriptions around the publication itself.

Move when the newsletter is the product and you want growth/monetization features closer to the inbox experience. Stay on Kit if your business depends on multi-step sequences, heavy tagging, or course/product funnels you are not ready to rebuild elsewhere.

  • How much of your Kit setup is periodic newsletters vs automated sequences?
  • Whether ads, boosts, or paid subscriptions matter more than ESP depth
  • Capacity to rebuild welcome flows and key tags after import

Read beehiiv vs Kit and the platform comparison if you are still choosing.

2. What will not transfer one-to-one

  • Sequences / automations: Expect to rebuild welcome series and key flows in beehiiv (or a connected tool), not import them.
  • Tags and rules: Subscriber lists/exports usually move; complex tag logic and visual automation graphs do not.
  • Forms and landing pages: Recreate embeds and pages; do not assume Kit form URLs keep working forever after cancel.
  • Integrations: Zapier/Make/webhooks and commerce syncs need rewiring.
  • Historical send analytics: Keep Kit exports or screenshots if you need old campaign stats.

3. Prepare accounts and backups

  1. Create a beehiiv publication on Launch (or your intended plan) and confirm you can send a test.
  2. In Kit, export a full subscriber CSV (and any segment/tag exports you rely on). Store a dated backup offline.
  3. List every live form, landing page, and “subscribe” button that still points at Kit.
  4. Note custom sending domain / DNS records currently used with Kit so you can re-auth on beehiiv.
  5. Freeze big list changes for a short window if you can — fewer mid-migration edge cases.

4. Export from Kit / ConvertKit

Exact menu labels change; the goal is a CSV with email, name fields you use, and status (subscribed/unsubscribed) where available.

  • Export all active subscribers you intend to keep mailing.
  • Export unsubscribes/suppressions if Kit provides them separately — honor them on import.
  • Optionally export tagged cohorts you will recreate as beehiiv segments or custom fields.
  • Do not import people who never opted in; stick to your existing permission basis.

5. Import into beehiiv

  1. Use beehiiv’s subscriber import for your CSV. Map email and name fields carefully.
  2. Suppress or exclude known unsubscribes so you do not re-mail people who opted out.
  3. Send a small internal test after import before any broadcast to the full list.
  4. Spot-check a few known addresses (you, a teammate, a secondary inbox).

If beehiiv warns about duplicates or formatting, fix the CSV and re-import cleanly rather than stacking partial loads.

6. Rebuild sequences and forms

  • Rebuild the welcome / onboarding series you actually use (not every Kit automation).
  • Replace Kit forms and embed codes on your site, Link-in-bio, and course thank-you pages.
  • Reconnect critical Zaps or webhooks to beehiiv events.
  • If you sold digital products through Kit commerce, plan the new checkout path before cutover day.

7. Domain, DNS, and branding

  • Authenticate your sending domain in beehiiv (SPF/DKIM as their docs require).
  • Update custom newsletter domain or publication URL settings if you use them.
  • Align logo, from-name, reply-to, and footer legal text with your brand.
  • Remove or update old Kit DNS entries only after beehiiv auth is verified with test sends.

8. Cutover and communication

  1. Pick a cutover window. Stop dual-sending the same newsletter from Kit and beehiiv.
  2. Tell subscribers once, plainly: same newsletter, new delivery home — no action needed if they already opted in.
  3. Point every public subscribe CTA at beehiiv before you cancel Kit billing.
  4. Keep Kit read-only for a short grace period in case you need one more export.
  5. Watch bounces and complaints on the first two beehiiv sends.

9. Final checklist

  • Subscriber CSV backed up and imported
  • Unsubscribes honored
  • Sending domain authenticated on beehiiv
  • Welcome (and any critical) sequences rebuilt
  • Site/forms pointed at beehiiv
  • Integrations rewired or intentionally dropped
  • Audience notified; Kit dual-send stopped
  • First live beehiiv send tested

If newsletter growth and monetization matter more than Kit’s ESP depth, beehiiv is the path this guide assumes.
