Which platform fits course creators

If you teach — courses, cohorts, workshops — your newsletter is usually a funnel, not just a magazine. Here’s how beehiiv, Kit, Ghost, and Substack typically fit that job.

Guide · September 2026 · Full comparison · Disclosure

What course creators usually need

  • Segmentation — lead magnet subscribers vs buyers vs alumni
  • Automations — welcome sequences, launch carts, win-backs
  • Commerce hooks — checkout links, tags when someone buys
  • A place to publish — free lessons, launch posts, updates
  • List ownership — so you’re not trapped if the course stack changes

No newsletter tool replaces a full course platform (Teachable, Kajabi, Maven, Circle, custom LMS, etc.). You’re choosing the email + publishing layer.

Short verdict

  • Default for most course funnels: Kit — automations and creator commerce are the point.
  • Audience growth + newsletter ads as a second revenue line: beehiiv — stronger growth/ad tooling; pair with an external course host.
  • Branded school site + membership content: Ghost — great owned site and paid members; bring your own automation depth.
  • Simple paid writing that occasionally mentions a course: Substack — fine for announcements; weak as a launch ESP.

Kit (ConvertKit)

Kit is built around creator email: forms, tags, visual automations, and selling digital products. If your mental model is “free newsletter → nurture → open cart → tag buyers,” Kit usually feels native.

Fits well when: launches, evergreen funnels, and segment-based sends matter more than a glossy magazine site.

You’ll still want: a course host (or Kit commerce for simpler digital products) and often a separate marketing site.

beehiiv

beehiiv shines when the newsletter itself is a growth and monetization engine (referrals, ads, boosts). Course creators use it successfully — but plan to connect an external course/checkout stack and accept that ESP-style automation may be lighter than Kit’s.

Fits well when: list growth and sponsorships matter alongside course launches.

You’ll still want: clear tagging/automation strategy and a dedicated course platform.

Ghost

Ghost gives you a real publication website plus memberships via Stripe. Excellent if your “course” is closer to a paid content library or member community on your own domain. For multi-step launch automations, creators often add an ESP or keep automations light.

Fits well when: ownership, design, and a branded member site are non-negotiable.

You’ll still want: a plan for sequences (native limits vs external ESP) and possibly a separate LMS for structured cohorts.

Substack

Substack is ideal for writing and paid subscriptions with network discovery. As a course-launch machine it’s limited: weak automations, limited tagging, and commerce that isn’t built like an ESP. Many educators keep Substack for essays and run launches elsewhere — or migrate when launches become core.

Fits well when: the course is secondary to a paid writing practice.

Consider migrating when: you need funnels, not just announcements. See Substack → beehiiv or evaluate Kit if automations are the bottleneck.

Decision shortcuts

If you care most about… Look at…
Launch sequences & tags Kit
Growing the list + newsletter ads beehiiv
Owned member site Ghost
Simple paid writing + occasional course mention Substack

Practical stack examples

  • Kit + course host — classic creator stack for launches.
  • beehiiv + Stripe/course host — newsletter growth engine feeding a separate school.
  • Ghost + Stripe (+ optional ESP) — owned media site with paid access; automate elsewhere if needed.
  • Substack alone — fine until launches need real segmentation.
