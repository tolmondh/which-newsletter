1. Decide if migration is worth it

Ghost is a publishing CMS: owned site, themes, memberships via Stripe, and long-term control (Ghost(Pro) or self-host). beehiiv is newsletter-first: publishing cadence, growth loops, referrals, ads/boosts, and paid subscriptions around the publication itself — not a full magazine CMS.

Move when the email newsletter is the product and you want growth/monetization features closer to the inbox. Stay on Ghost if you need a branded publication website, CMS workflows, or Stripe memberships tightly tied to on-site content you are not ready to give up.

  • How much of your Ghost value is the website vs the email list?
  • Whether ads, boosts, or beehiiv-style paid subs matter more than an owned CMS
  • How many paid members you have and whether Stripe cutover capacity exists

Read the platform comparison and Substack alternatives if you are still choosing between owned-site and newsletter-first stacks.

2. Honest caveats (site, paid, content)

Expect friction here. Do not assume a clean lift-and-shift.

  • The Ghost website does not transfer. Themes, pages, navigation, and the public CMS archive stay on Ghost (or go offline when you cancel). beehiiv gives you a publication presence and email — not a Ghost-equivalent owned magazine site.
  • Posts and newsletters are not a one-click port. You may export content for archive, but plan to republish key issues in beehiiv (or keep a static archive elsewhere) rather than expecting perfect HTML/theme fidelity.
  • Paid members need a deliberate Stripe plan. Ghost memberships typically run through Stripe. beehiiv has its own paid-subscription path. Moving paying customers means cancel/re-subscribe coordination, proration questions, and clear communication — not a silent CSV trick.
  • Self-host vs Ghost(Pro) differs. Export locations and member tools vary. Follow Ghost’s current docs for your hosting path.
  • Compliance still applies. Preserve unsubscribes and suppressions. Do not re-permission people who opted out.

3. Prepare accounts and backups

  1. Create and verify your beehiiv publication Pick the plan that matches list size and features you need (custom domain, ads, paid, etc.). Confirm sending-domain requirements early.
  2. Inventory your Ghost setup Free members vs paid, active Stripe products/prices, custom sending domain, critical posts you want to keep public, and every “subscribe” CTA on the site.
  3. Download backups offline Member exports, a content/JSON or post export if available, notes on Stripe price IDs, and copies of essential email/welcome copy you will rebuild.
  4. Decide the fate of the Ghost site Keep it as a static archive, redirect key URLs, or shut it down after cutover — pick before you cancel billing.

4. Export from Ghost

Typical goals (verify in Ghost’s current members/content export UI for Ghost(Pro) or self-host):

  • Export members with email, name, status (free/paid/comped where available), and labels/tiers you still care about.
  • Export or separately save suppressed/unsubscribed addresses so you do not re-mail them.
  • Export content/JSON if you want an offline archive of posts — treat it as backup, not a beehiiv import format.
  • From Stripe (not only Ghost), note active subscriptions tied to your Ghost products so paid cutover is intentional.

Clean CSVs before import: normalize emails, drop junk rows, and keep a clear free-vs-paid vs suppressed column.

5. Import into beehiiv

  1. Import free/newsletter subscribers first Use beehiiv’s subscriber import. Map fields carefully. Tag former Ghost cohorts you still need (e.g. “was-paid”, “founding”, “vip”).
  2. Do not blindly import active paid Ghost members as free beehiiv paid Paid access must follow beehiiv’s subscription setup and your Stripe/customer communication plan (next section).
  3. Respect opt-in and local email rules Follow beehiiv’s guidance and your jurisdiction. A warm “we’re moving homes” note beats a sudden blast from a new domain.
  4. Send small tests first Authenticate sending, then deliver to yourself and a few trusted inboxes before any mass send.

6. Paid members and Stripe

This is the highest-risk part of a Ghost → beehiiv move. Verify current Stripe and beehiiv paid-subscription docs; do not invent a silent transfer.

  • Inventory active Ghost/Stripe subscriptions — product names, prices, renewal dates, comps.
  • Choose a cutover policy — e.g. finish the current Ghost billing period, then invite members to re-subscribe on beehiiv; or offer a timed complimentary beehiiv paid period while people switch. Pick what you can support in support tickets.
  • Communicate early and twice — why you’re moving, what happens to their access, and the exact re-subscribe link when ready.
  • Cancel Ghost membership products only after beehiiv paid is live and you’ve handled the cohort that should not lose access mid-cycle.
  • Keep Stripe access until refunds, disputes, and final invoices are settled.

7. Rebuild content, forms, and growth

  • Welcome / onboarding: Recreate a short welcome sequence in beehiiv — one clear first email often beats a complex Ghost membership drip you never maintained.
  • Key posts: Republish evergreen issues worth keeping live; link an archive or keep the Ghost site read-only if URLs matter for SEO.
  • Signup forms & embeds: Replace Ghost portal/subscribe CTAs on any external sites, bios, and course thank-you pages.
  • Referral / growth features: If growth was why you left Ghost, turn on beehiiv’s referral and related tools after the list is stable.
  • Integrations: Rewire Zapier/Make/webhooks that pointed at Ghost member events.

8. Domain, DNS, and branding

  • Add SPF/DKIM (and DMARC as recommended) for beehiiv’s sending domain at your DNS host.
  • If Ghost used the same root or subdomain for the site and email, plan carefully — site redirects and email auth can conflict if you rush DNS edits.
  • Update website CTAs, footers, and “subscribe” buttons away from Ghost portal links.
  • Keep Ghost paused-but-accessible during a short overlap for member exports and Stripe cleanup.

9. Cutover and communication

  1. Announce early Tell free and paid members you’re moving email homes, what (if anything) they need to do, and that preferences/unsubscribes still work.
  2. Stop dual-sending the same content Pick a date. After cutover, Ghost is archive/membership wind-down only — not a second blast channel to the same people.
  3. Send the first beehiiv issue Include a short “you’re here now” note, how to update preferences, paid re-subscribe instructions if needed, and a reply-friendly support address.
  4. Watch deliverability and support Monitor bounces, complaints, and paid-access tickets; fix auth issues immediately; suppress hard bounces.

Final checklist

  • Member + unsubscribe/suppression exports saved offline
  • Content/JSON or post archive saved if you need it
  • Free list imported; must-have tags rebuilt
  • Paid/Stripe cutover policy decided and communicated
  • Sending domain authenticated on beehiiv
  • Welcome (and any critical) automations rebuilt — not assumed transferred
  • Site/forms/embeds pointed at beehiiv; Ghost site fate decided
  • Integrations rewired or intentionally dropped
  • Audience notified; Ghost dual-send stopped
  • First live beehiiv send tested

If a newsletter-first stack is the goal — growth tools, ads/sponsorships, and simpler ops than running a CMS — beehiiv is the path this guide assumes. Stay on Ghost when the owned publication website is non-negotiable.
