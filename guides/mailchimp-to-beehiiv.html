1. Decide if migration is worth it

Mailchimp is a full email service provider: audiences, nested segments, journeys, ecommerce connectors, and agency-style reporting. beehiiv is built around the newsletter as the product — publishing, growth loops, referrals, ads/sponsorship tooling, and paid subscriptions.

Move when you care more about newsletter growth and monetization than Mailchimp’s ESP depth. Stay (or pick Kit/Ghost) if your business runs on complex automations, heavy tagging, or ecommerce syncs you are not ready to rebuild.

  • How much of your Mailchimp setup is “newsletter sends” vs multi-step journeys?
  • Whether ads, boosts, or paid subscriptions matter more than advanced segmentation
  • Team capacity to rebuild welcome flows and segments after import

Read the platform comparison and beehiiv vs Kit if you are still choosing between newsletter-first and ESP-first tools.

2. Honest caveats (segments & automations)

Expect friction here. Do not assume a clean lift-and-shift.

  • Segmentation models differ. Mailchimp audiences, tags, groups, and nested segments often do not map 1:1 to beehiiv’s segments/tags. Export what you need as columns or separate CSVs, then rebuild the segments that still matter on beehiiv.
  • Automations and journeys do not transfer. Welcome series, abandoned-cart style flows, and branch logic stay in Mailchimp. Budget time to recreate only the flows you actually use — often a simple welcome + a few nurture emails is enough on a newsletter stack.
  • Templates and campaigns are not portable as-is. Redesign key templates in beehiiv rather than fighting HTML paste jobs.
  • Integrations need rewiring. Forms, Zapier/Make hooks, Shopify/CRM syncs, and signup embeds point at Mailchimp today — update destinations before you stop Mailchimp sends.
  • Compliance and suppressions still apply. Preserve unsubscribes and bounced addresses. Do not re-permission people who opted out.

3. Prepare accounts and backups

  1. Create and verify your beehiiv publication Pick the plan that matches list size and features you need (custom domain, ads, paid, etc.). Confirm sending-domain requirements early.
  2. Inventory your Mailchimp account Audiences in use, critical segments/tags, active journeys, signup forms, connected apps, and which campaigns are “must keep” for archive reference.
  3. Download backups offline Contact exports, suppression/unsubscribe lists, a note of custom fields, and copies of essential email copy you will rebuild.

4. Export from Mailchimp

Typical path (verify in Mailchimp’s current audience/export UI):

  • Export subscribed contacts from each audience you intend to migrate.
  • Export or separately save unsubscribed and cleaned addresses so you do not re-mail them.
  • Include useful fields: email, first name, signup source/date if available, tags or group memberships you still care about.
  • If you rely on many segments, export segment memberships as extra columns or separate files — you will rebuild logic later.

Clean the CSV before import: normalize emails, drop obvious junk rows, and keep a clear status column (subscribed vs suppressed).

5. Import into beehiiv

  1. Import via beehiiv’s subscriber import tools Map fields carefully. Tag former Mailchimp segments that you still need (e.g. “vip”, “course-buyer”, “free-only”).
  2. Respect opt-in and local email rules Follow beehiiv’s guidance and your jurisdiction. A warm “we’re moving homes” note beats a sudden blast from a new domain.
  3. Send small tests first Authenticate sending, then deliver to yourself and a few trusted inboxes before any mass send.

6. Rebuild what Mailchimp won’t move

  • Welcome / onboarding: Recreate a short welcome sequence in beehiiv — one clear first email often beats a 12-step journey you never maintained.
  • Segments that still earn their keep: Rebuild only high-value splits (paid vs free, product buyers, geography if you use it). Drop zombie segments.
  • Signup forms & embeds: Replace Mailchimp forms on your site, Linktree, course thank-you pages, and social bios.
  • Referral / growth features: If growth was why you left Mailchimp, turn on beehiiv’s referral and related tools after the list is stable.
  • Paid offers: If you sell subscriptions or digital products via email, confirm beehiiv’s paid/subscription path (or keep checkout elsewhere and use beehiiv for the list).

7. Domain, DNS, and branding

  • Add SPF/DKIM (and DMARC as recommended) for beehiiv’s sending domain at your DNS host.
  • If Mailchimp authenticated the same root or subdomain, plan the cutover so you do not break both senders at once — stagger or use distinct sending subdomains when possible.
  • Update website CTAs, footers, and “subscribe” buttons away from Mailchimp forms.
  • Keep Mailchimp paused-but-accessible during a short overlap for suppressions and last exports.

8. Cutover and communication

  1. Announce early Tell subscribers you’re moving email homes, what (if anything) they need to do, and that preferences/unsubscribes still work.
  2. Stop dual-sending the same content Pick a date. After cutover, Mailchimp is archive/suppression only — not a second blast channel to the same people.
  3. Send the first beehiiv issue Include a short “you’re here now” note, how to update preferences, and a reply-friendly support address.
  4. Watch deliverability Monitor bounces and complaints; fix auth issues immediately; suppress hard bounces.

Final checklist

  • Contact + unsubscribe/suppression exports saved offline
  • Import complete; must-have tags/segments rebuilt
  • Sending domain authenticated on beehiiv
  • Welcome (and any critical) automations rebuilt — not assumed transferred
  • Site/forms/embeds pointed at beehiiv
  • Integrations rewired or intentionally dropped
  • Audience notified; Mailchimp dual-send stopped
  • First live beehiiv send tested

If a newsletter-first stack is the goal — growth tools, ads/sponsorships, and simpler ops than a full ESP — beehiiv is the path this guide assumes.
