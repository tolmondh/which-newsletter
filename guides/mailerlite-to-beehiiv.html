1. Decide if migration is worth it

MailerLite is an affordable, all-round email marketing tool: forms, pop-ups, landing pages, automations, and campaigns. beehiiv is built around the newsletter as the product: publishing, a hosted publication site and archive, referral and recommendation growth, and monetization through ads, Boosts, sponsorships, and paid subscriptions.

Moving usually makes sense when:

  • Your MailerLite account is mostly a regular newsletter, not store promos or lead-nurture funnels.
  • You keep hand-building newsletter features (web archive, referral rewards, sponsor slots) that beehiiv includes.
  • You want ads, Boosts, sponsorships, or paid subscriptions to become real revenue lines.

Stay put if email mainly supports a shop or service, you depend on MailerLite’s store or site-builder integrations, or your small list works fine and switching would cost time without unlocking anything you’ll use. Still deciding? Read beehiiv vs MailerLite first.

2. Honest caveats

Expect some friction. Don’t assume a clean lift-and-shift.

  • Automations don’t transfer. MailerLite workflows (welcome series, triggers on group joins, conditional branches) stay in MailerLite. Rebuild only the flows you actually use.
  • Groups and segments need mapping. MailerLite groups and segments won’t become beehiiv tags or segments automatically. Carry what you need as CSV columns or separate files and rebuild the useful ones.
  • Landing pages, pop-ups, and MailerLite-hosted sites stay behind. Any page or form hosted by MailerLite needs a replacement: a beehiiv signup form or page, or your own site.
  • Campaign history and templates aren’t portable as-is. Save copies of issues you want to keep, and redesign your main template in beehiiv rather than pasting old HTML.
  • Integrations need rewiring. Store connections, Zapier/Make hooks, and site-builder embeds point at MailerLite today. Update or retire each one on purpose.
  • Suppressions still apply. Unsubscribed, bounced, and complaint addresses must stay suppressed. Never re-permission people who opted out.

3. Inventory your MailerLite account

  1. List what’s actually in use Active groups, saved segments, custom fields, live automations, embedded forms and pop-ups, landing pages, and connected integrations.
  2. Mark each item keep, rebuild, or drop Many accounts carry old groups and paused workflows nobody touches. Migration is a good time to retire them.
  3. Note anything paid or transactional Paid newsletter subscriptions, digital product sales, or store receipts tied to MailerLite need their own plan (see section 8).
  4. Create the beehiiv publication Set branding, sender name, reply-to address, and a welcome email before importing anyone.

4. Export from MailerLite

Typical path (verify in MailerLite’s current subscribers/export UI; older MailerLite Classic accounts may label things differently):

  • Export active subscribers as CSV with the fields you need: email, name, signup date, and any custom fields you’ll keep.
  • Export unsubscribed, bounced, and junk/complaint contacts separately so they’re never imported as active.
  • If groups matter, export each important group separately or make sure group membership appears as a column.
  • Save copies of key campaign content and your welcome emails so you can rebuild them.

Clean the CSV before import: normalize email casing, remove obvious junk rows and duplicates, and keep one clear status column. Store the raw exports offline as a backup.

5. Map groups, segments, and fields

Build a simple mapping table before you import. For example:

  • MailerLite group → beehiiv tag or custom field. Keep only groups you’ll actually target (e.g. “customers”, “workshop attendees”, “local readers”).
  • MailerLite segment → rebuilt beehiiv segment. Segments are rules, not lists. Recreate the rule in beehiiv where an equivalent field exists; otherwise export the current members as a tag.
  • Custom fields → beehiiv custom fields. Create the fields in beehiiv first so the importer can map them.
  • Source tag. Add a tag like “migrated-mailerlite” so you can tell migrated readers from new signups later.

6. Import into beehiiv

  1. Use beehiiv’s subscriber import Upload the cleaned active CSV, map columns to fields, and apply your tags. Check beehiiv’s current docs for import limits or review steps on your plan.
  2. Decide on the welcome email Choose whether imported readers should get a welcome email. Usually a single “we’ve moved” note is better than putting everyone through a new-subscriber sequence.
  3. Respect consent rules Import only people who opted in, follow beehiiv’s import policy and the email laws where you and your readers live.
  4. Spot-check counts Compare the imported total and a few tagged records against your MailerLite export before sending anything.

7. Rebuild automations, forms, and pages

  • Welcome / onboarding: Recreate a short welcome flow in beehiiv. One strong first email plus a couple of follow-ups often beats a sprawling workflow nobody maintains. Check which automation features your beehiiv plan includes.
  • Forms and pop-ups: Replace MailerLite embeds on your website, link-in-bio pages, and thank-you pages with beehiiv signup forms or links to your beehiiv publication.
  • Landing pages: Point old MailerLite landing page links at your beehiiv publication or a page on your own site. Update any QR codes or printed links.
  • Marketing workflows you still need: If you rely on store-triggered or multi-branch automations, decide honestly whether beehiiv covers them. If not, you may keep a separate tool for those flows and use beehiiv for the newsletter.
  • Growth features: Once the list is stable, turn on beehiiv’s referral program and recommendations if they fit your plan. See how to monetize a newsletter on beehiiv for what to enable and when.

8. Paid newsletters and Stripe

If readers pay you through MailerLite’s paid newsletter or selling features (typically via Stripe), slow down here:

  • Find out how your current subscriptions are billed and whether the Stripe account can connect to beehiiv. Check both vendors’ docs for what migrates and what doesn’t.
  • Don’t promise paying readers uninterrupted access until you’ve confirmed the billing path and tested it.
  • Avoid double-billing: make sure nobody gets charged by both systems after cutover.
  • Tell paid readers in plain language what changes for them, if anything, before you switch.

9. Domain, DNS, and sender reputation

  • Add the SPF/DKIM records beehiiv asks for (and a DMARC record if you don’t have one) at your DNS host.
  • If MailerLite authenticated the same domain, leave its records in place until cutover is finished, then remove the ones you no longer need. Watch for conflicting SPF records: a domain should publish a single SPF record.
  • Keep the same sender name and a familiar from-address so readers recognize you in the inbox.
  • For larger lists, consider sending the first beehiiv issues to your most engaged readers first, and check beehiiv’s guidance on warming up.

10. Cutover and communication

  1. Announce from MailerLite first (optional) A short heads-up from your current sender that the newsletter is moving helps readers recognize the new emails.
  2. Pick a cutover date and stop dual-sending After that date, MailerLite is for archive and suppression reference only, not a second channel to the same people.
  3. Send the first beehiiv issue Include a brief “you’re in the right place” note, an easy unsubscribe, and a reply address for questions.
  4. Watch the first few sends Monitor bounces, complaints, and replies. Fix authentication issues right away and suppress hard bounces.
  5. Downgrade MailerLite last Keep the account (or full exports) until you’re confident nothing is missing, then downgrade or close it.

Final checklist

  • Active, unsubscribed, and bounced exports saved offline
  • Group/segment/field mapping table written down
  • Import complete, tagged “migrated-mailerlite”, counts spot-checked
  • Sending domain authenticated on beehiiv; single SPF record
  • Welcome and must-keep automations rebuilt, not assumed transferred
  • Forms, pop-ups, landing page links, and bios pointed at beehiiv
  • Integrations rewired or retired on purpose
  • Paid subscriber billing path confirmed (if applicable)
  • Readers notified; MailerLite dual-send stopped
  • First beehiiv issue tested and sent

If a newsletter-first stack is the goal (built-in growth tools, ad and sponsor revenue, and a publication site), beehiiv is the path this guide assumes. If you mainly need affordable marketing automation for a business, MailerLite may still be the better fit.
