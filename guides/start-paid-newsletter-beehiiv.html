Why beehiiv for paid

  • 0% platform cut positioning on paid subscription revenue (you still pay Stripe processing) — compare that to Substack’s percentage take-rate.
  • Software you can start free on Launch, then move to Scale when paid tools and list size require it — see pricing guide.
  • Same home for free + paid with growth and (on paid plans) ad tooling if you later add sponsorships.

Create your beehiiv account

Before you open an account

  • Niche and promise — one sentence: who it’s for and what paid readers get that free readers don’t.
  • Cadence you can keep — weekly free + monthly paid deep-dive is a common shape; pick something sustainable.
  • Price band — pick a simple monthly + annual. You can change later; don’t stall on perfection.
  • Legal/tax basics — business identity, Stripe identity checks, and any VAT/sales-tax questions for your situation.

Setup path (step by step)

  1. Create a beehiiv publication Start on Launch unless you already know you need paid-tier features immediately. Sign up via beehiiv.
  2. Name, about, and web presence Clear publication name, bio, and signup page. Custom domain when your plan allows — brand trust matters for paid.
  3. Authenticate sending Add SPF/DKIM (and DMARC as recommended) for your sending domain before any real audience mail. Test to yourself.
  4. Build the free list first A paid wall with nobody on the free list is a quiet room. Publish free issues; embed signup everywhere you already have attention.
  5. Connect Stripe / billing Complete beehiiv’s paid subscription setup and Stripe onboarding. Confirm currency, receipts, and customer portal behavior.
  6. Configure free vs paid content Decide which posts are public, email-only, or paywalled. Be consistent so buyers know what they’re buying.
  7. Upgrade plan if required Paid subscription tooling and related monetization features may require Scale (or higher). Confirm on beehiiv’s pricing page — don’t assume Launch unlocks everything.
  8. Welcome + billing emails Set expectations: what paid includes, how to cancel/manage, and how to get support.

Design the paid offer

Paid fails when free and paid feel identical. Make the upgrade obvious:

  • Extra depth — research notes, templates, private briefings.
  • Higher frequency — paid mid-week, free weekend — only if you can sustain it.
  • Access — archive, community, office hours, or downloadable assets.
  • Not just “early access to the same post” — weak differentiation drives churn.

Write the upgrade page in plain language: price, what’s included, what’s not, and refund/cancel basics.

Launch without embarrassing yourself

  1. Soft launch to superfans Personal notes beat a cold blast. Fix billing confusion before a wide announce.
  2. Announce on the free list One clear email: why paid exists, what they get, link to upgrade. No hype charts.
  3. Ship paid content on schedule The first 30 days teach buyers whether to stay. Missed cadence kills renewals.
  4. Add growth loops after the offer works Referrals, boosts, or ads — after the paid product is real. See beehiiv vs Substack if you’re migrating an existing paid base.

Checklist

  • Publication created; sending domain authenticated
  • Free signup live on site/social/bio
  • Stripe connected; test purchase completed
  • Paid vs free content rules documented
  • Upgrade page live with clear price and inclusions
  • Plan tier matches required monetization features
  • Support reply template for billing questions
  • First paid issue drafted before the announce
