Migrate from Substack to beehiiv

A practical outline of the real steps — not a magic one-click promise. Confirm each step against current Substack and beehiiv docs before you move a live list.

Guide outline · September 2026 · See also platform comparison

Disclaimer: Platforms change export/import UIs. This is a process map. Paid subscriptions and payment processors are the riskiest part — plan those carefully.

1. Decide if migration is worth it

Move when beehiiv’s growth/monetization tools matter more than Substack’s network and simplicity. Stay if discovery and low ops still win for you. Read the comparison first.

  • List size and growth goals
  • Whether you need ads/sponsorship tooling vs reader-paid only
  • How painful paid-subscriber migration will be for your audience

2. Prepare accounts and backups

  1. Create and verify your beehiiv publication Pick the plan you’ll need for your list size and features. Confirm sending domain requirements early.
  2. Inventory what you have on Substack Free vs paid subscribers, segments, posts you care about, custom links, and any integrations.
  3. Download everything you might need later Subscriber CSVs, post exports or copies, media, and a note of your publication URL and social links.

3. Export from Substack

Typical path (verify in Substack settings):

  • Export subscribers (often separate free and paid, or tagged in CSV).
  • Export or archive posts you want to republish.
  • Note unsubscribe and suppression considerations — don’t re-mail people who opted out.

Clean the CSV: emails, names, status (free/paid), signup date if available. Remove obvious bad rows.

4. Import into beehiiv

  1. Import subscribers via beehiiv’s import tools Map fields carefully. Tag or segment former Substack free vs paid if you can.
  2. Confirm double opt-in / compliance settings Follow beehiiv’s guidance and your local email laws. Don’t blast a cold import without a warm re-permission plan if required.
  3. Send a small test Deliverability check to yourself and a few trusted addresses before any mass send.

5. Handle paid subscribers

This is the hard part. Substack billing does not “move” automatically to beehiiv.

  • Decide: cancel-and-rejoin on beehiiv, grandfather rates, or run parallel for a transition window.
  • Communicate clearly: why you’re moving, what happens to access, how to resubscribe, and support contact.
  • Expect churn. Plan a grace period and a personal note to high-value subscribers.
  • Confirm Stripe (or beehiiv’s billing) setup, taxes, and receipts before you invite people to pay again.

6. Domain, DNS, and branding

  • If you use a custom domain or custom sending domain on beehiiv, add SPF/DKIM (and DMARC as recommended) at your DNS host.
  • Update website CTAs, Linktree, social bios, and email signatures away from the Substack URL over time.
  • Keep the Substack publication up with a pinned post pointing to the new home during transition.

7. Posts, archives, and redirects

  • Republish key posts in beehiiv if you want a living archive (or link out to Substack archives temporarily).
  • You usually cannot fully 301 every Substack URL onto beehiiv the way you’d migrate a self-hosted blog — plan for broken deep links and update your most shared URLs manually.
  • Rebuild welcome/onboarding emails and referral flows in beehiiv’s tools.

8. Cutover and communication

  1. Announce early Tell free and paid readers what’s changing and when. One clear email beats three vague ones.
  2. Pause Substack publishing Or publish only a redirect notice. Avoid dual-sending the same content to the same people.
  3. Send the first beehiiv issue Include a short “you’re here now” note and how to update preferences.
  4. Monitor bounces and complaints Fix DNS/auth issues immediately; suppress hard bounces.

Final checklist

  • Subscriber export saved offline
  • Import complete; segments tagged
  • Sending domain authenticated
  • Paid transition plan + support reply template
  • Welcome/automation rebuilt
  • Public links updated (site, social, bio)
  • Substack notice posted
  • First live send tested
